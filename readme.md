# ISAR Imaging based on Time-Frequency Analysis and Inverse Synthetic Aperture Radar Imaging Technology [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction  
Inverse Synthetic Aperture Radar (ISARD) imaging primarily utilizes several algorithms, including the Range-Doppler algorithm (RD), Polarization Formatted Aperture Algorithm (PFA), Convolution Back Projection method, and Super-Resolution Imaging Processing algorithms. Among these, the Range-Doppler algorithm is the most fundamental and practical for imaging, utilizing wideband signals to achieve high resolution in the range direction and multi-resolution in the Doppler frequency in the azimuth direction. However, it has certain limitations: 1) It requires a sustained observation period to obtain images with higher horizontal resolution; 2) During the observation period, the target scattering points do not experience range cell motion; 3) The target is flying steadily, with no change in Doppler frequency [1]. However, moving targets exhibit time-varying Doppler frequencies during observation, which would cause degradation of azimuth resolution if Fourier transform is used, leading to image blurriness. If time-frequency transformation is employed instead of Fourier transform, different Doppler frequencies at different moments can be analyzed, thus obtaining the target's range-time Doppler image.
The study of time-frequency analysis began in the 1940s, focusing mainly on non-stationary or time-varying signals, with the main task being to describe the spectral components of signals changing over time. It effectively handles time-varying signals, making it applicable in acquiring ISARD motion compensation information, instantaneous imaging, ISARD imaging post-processing, and understanding imaging principles. This paper applies time-frequency analysis techniques to ISARD imaging to reflect the target's instantaneous image, analyzes the impact of different time-frequency analysis functions on imaging, and successfully applies time-frequency analysis technology to motion compensation in ISARD imaging of maneuvering targets.
